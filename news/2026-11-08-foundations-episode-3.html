Foundations, Week Three: Making It Real

The Foundations series continues tonight, Sunday, November 8, at 7:00 PM ET, with Episode 3, “Making It Real: Structure, Registration, and the Rules.” Episode 1 defined a business, and Episode 2 found the idea and the customer and tested whether anyone would pay. Tonight the idea becomes a legal thing: a shape on paper, a name the state recognizes, a tax number, the permissions your town and your industry require, and a bank account with the business’s name on it, not yours.

The episode starts from the U.S. Small Business Administration’s own framing: the business structure you choose influences everything from day-to-day operations, to taxes, and how much of your personal assets are at risk. Alex and Jordan work through the four main shapes in the order most owners meet them — sole proprietorship, partnership, limited liability company, and corporation, including the S corporation election — and then run the four questions that let the answers pick the shape: risk, ownership, tax, and the future you intend for the business.

Then comes the registration sequence, in order: choose the structure before you register, sort out the legal name and any doing-business-as name, make the state filing, get the federal Employer Identification Number — free, directly from the IRS — handle state taxes, licenses, and permits, and open a separate business bank account so the liability wall you formed on paper survives in practice. The episode also updates a rule that changed this year: on August 11, 2026, the Treasury Department announced a final rule, effective August 14, permanently removing the federal beneficial-ownership reporting requirement for United States companies under the Corporate Transparency Act.

Takeaway: Structure is not a label. It decides who pays when something goes wrong, how the money gets taxed, and how much administration you owe every year whether business is good or bad. Choose it before you register, keep the business’s money separate from yours, and do not let the paperwork become the project.
