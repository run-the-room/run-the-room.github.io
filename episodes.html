Starting From Zero: What a Business Actually Is

October 25, 2026 · 22:37

▶ Play episode
Episode notes & sources

Starting From Zero opens Run the Room at the true beginning: what a business actually is. Alex and Jordan define a business as an organized answer to somebody else's problem, offered in exchange for money, with the machinery to keep that exchange going — then test that definition against Peter Drucker's 1954 claim that the purpose of a business is to create a customer, the three legs of offer, customers, and money, Bureau of Labor Statistics survival data, cash-flow research, and four common impostors: a hobby, a job you buy yourself, an audience, and a product without a system. New episodes every Sunday. Run the Room builds business from the very beginning — foundations first, one step each week.

Sources:
- Peter Drucker, The Practice of Management (1954), p. 37: "There is only one valid definition of business purpose: to create a customer" — https://en.wikiquote.org/w/index.php?title=Peter_Drucker&oldid=2975895
- U.S. Small Business Administration, Launch your business guide (structure, registration, tax IDs, licenses): https://www.sba.gov/es/guia-de-negocios/lance-su-empresa
- U.S. Small Business Administration, Choose a business structure (updated March 7, 2025): https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/launch-your-business/choose-business-structure
- Bureau of Labor Statistics, Business Employment Dynamics establishment survival through March 2025 (77.9% year 1, 51.4% at 5 years for the 2020 cohort, 34.7% at 10 years for the 2015 cohort), summarized at: https://startbusinessbystate.com/business-survival-rates-by-state/
- SCORE / U.S. Bank study cited on cash flow and small business failure (about 82% tied to poor cash flow management), and Federal Reserve 2026 Report on Employer Firms based on the 2025 Small Business Credit Survey: https://www.oneparkfinancial.com/blog/why-82-percent-of-small-businesses-fail-because-of-cash-flow
